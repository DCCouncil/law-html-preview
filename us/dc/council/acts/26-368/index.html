D.C. Act 26-368. Leading Education Access for Reentry and Necessary Success ("LEARNS") Amendment Act of 2026.

Law not yet effective

This act is not yet codified in the D.C. Code because it is not yet effective as law. It is projected to become effective on Dec. 12, 2026.

AN ACT

To amend An Act To create a Department of Corrections in the District of Columbia to designate the Department of Corrections as the District agency to provide a free appropriate public education under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act and District law to certain individuals in its custody and detained in its secure facilities, to expand eligibility for special education assessments for individuals in its custody, and to require that the Department of Corrections establish an online portal and tracking system for grievances and short-term and long-term service requests; to repeal parts of section 3001 of Subtitle A of Title 5 of the District of Columbia Municipal Regulations; to amend Chapter 3 of Title 14 of the District of Columbia Official Code to clarify that certain provisions only apply to a victim who is under the age of 18; and for other purposes.

BE IT ENACTED BY THE COUNCIL OF THE DISTRICT OF COLUMBIA, That this act may be cited as the "Leading Education Access for Reentry and Necessary Success ("LEARNS") Amendment Act of 2026".

Sec. 2. An Act To create a Department of Corrections in the District of Columbia, approved June 27, 1946 (60 Stat. 320; D.C. Official Code § 24-211.01 et seq.), is amended as follows:

(a) Section 2(b) (D.C. Official Code § 24-211.02(b)) is amended as follows:

(1) Paragraph (9) is amended by striking the phrase "; and" and inserting a semicolon in its place.

(2) The first paragraph (10) is amended by striking the period and inserting a semicolon in its place.

(3) The second paragraph (10) is redesignated as paragraph (11).

(4) Paragraph (11) is amended by striking the period and inserting the phrase "; and" in its place.

(5) A new paragraph (12) is added to read as follows:

"(12) During each school year:

"(A) Provide a free appropriate public education under the IDEA and District law, to individuals with disabilities who are in its custody and detained in its secure facilities from the age of 18 through the end of the individual's eligibility for special education and related services under the IDEA and District law; and

"(B) Following a request for evaluation by the individual or a person authorized to request an evaluation pursuant to 5-A DCMR § 3004, shall evaluate individuals in its custody and detained in its secure facilities, who are age 18 through the end of the individual's eligibility for special education and related services under the IDEA and District law, and who were not previously assessed for special education services, to determine if the individual is a child with a disability.".

(b) New sections 11a, 11b, and 11c are added to read as follows:

"Sec. 11a. Education services.

"(a) The Department of Corrections, as the agency responsible for ensuring a free appropriate public education, shall make a free appropriate public education available to all students with a disability from age 18 through the end of the individual's eligibility for special education and related services under the IDEA and District law who are in the custody of the Department of Corrections, including students who, in the last educational placement prior to their incarceration in an adult correctional facility, were not identified as being a child with a disability in accordance with Chapter 30 of Subtitle A of Title 5 of the District of Columbia Municipal Regulations (5-A DCMR § 3001 et seq.) ("Chapter 30"), or who did not have an individualized education program in accordance with Chapter 30.

"(b) The Department of Corrections shall contract for the delivery of education services necessary to provide free appropriate public education to students eligible under this section. The contracted education provider shall, at a minimum:

"(1) Have experience and expertise in providing education in the District;

"(2) Manage and run a high school diploma program within the Department of Corrections that offers credit-bearing courses and post-secondary planning and preparation;

"(3) Be able to provide in-person instruction to every eligible student, including those in segregated or restricted housing and on medical units;

"(4) Understand the unique social, emotional, and learning needs of incarcerated students; and

"(5) Be able to implement every student's individualized education program.

"Sec. 11b. Grievance and service request tracking system.

"(a) Beginning January 1, 2027, the Department of Corrections ("Department") shall establish and maintain on its website an online portal, which shall include a grievance and service request tracking system, that shall:

"(1) Be accessible to individuals in the Department's custody for the purpose of submitting, tracking, and receiving updates on service requests, grievances, and complaints through the tracking system;

"(2) Provide a public facing, view-only interface that allows members of the public to search and view aggregate information from the tracking system, but does not permit the public to submit, modify, or otherwise input service requests, grievances, or complaints;

"(3) Provide individuals in the Department's custody with access to:

"(A) Contact information for the Director, Deputy Director of Education, Programs, and Case Management, Deputy Director of Operations, and other agency leadership responsible for receiving, tracking, and resolving grievances, as well as the Corrections Information Council and Council Committee with oversight of the Department;

"(B) Information regarding the number of service requests, grievances, or complaints to staff made by the individual in the Department's custody, including those related to Council proceedings, available resources, and access to the Advisory Neighborhood Commissioner;

"(C) A list of the rights of each individual residing in the correctional facility; and

"(D) Instructions on how individuals in the Department's custody may submit a service request, grievance, or complaint and track the service request, grievance, or complaint in the tracking system, including viewing real-time updates posted by the Department to the tracking system.

"(b)(1) By July 1, 2027, the Department shall assign each service request, grievance, or complaint submitted to the tracking system a unique ticket number.

"(2) Each ticket shall be searchable through the tracking system described in subsection (a) of this section by ticket number, date of submission, status, and the nature of the service request, grievance, or complaint.

"(3) The public-facing portal shall allow members of the public to search and view aggregate ticket data including the ticket number, date of submission, and status and nature of the service request, grievance, or complaint, but shall not allow the public to access personally identifiable information or to submit, alter, or interact with any ticket.

"(4) The tracking system shall provide for each submission:

"(A) The nature of the service request, grievance, or complaint;

"(B) The date of submission;

"(C) The current status of the service request, grievance, or complaint;

"(D) Whether or not the service request, grievance, or complaint is open or closed; and

"(E) Information on how the individual or their family member may contact the Department to receive additional information on the status or resolution of the service request, grievance, or complaint.

"(5) The Department may prescribe the format in which submissions to the tracking system are to be made; provided, that the Department shall accept submissions made by individuals in the Department's custody in writing and via oral communication.

"(c) Within 30 days after the launch of the tracking system pursuant to subsection (b)(1) of this section, the Department shall input into the tracking system all service requests, grievances, and complaints received by the Department in the preceding 6 months that are open.

"Sec. 11c. Definitions.

" For the purposes of this act, the term:

"(1) "Free appropriate public education" shall have the same meaning as provided in section 101(3) of the Placement of Students with Disabilities in Nonpublic Schools Amendment Act of 2006, effective March 14, 2007 (D.C. Law 16-269; D.C. Official Code § 38-2561.01(3)).

"(2) "IDEA" shall have the same meaning as provided in section 101(4) of the Placement of Students with Disabilities in Nonpublic Schools Amendment Act of 2006, effective March 14, 2007 (D.C. Law 16-269; D.C. Official Code § 38-2561.01(4)).

"(3) "Individualized education program" means a written plan that specifies the special education programs and services to be provided to meet the unique educational needs of a child with a disability, as required under section 614(d) of the IDEA (20 U.S.C. § 1414(d)).".

Sec. 5. Title 20 of the District of Columbia Official Code is amended as follows:

(a) Section 20-311 is amended to read as follows:

"Sec. 20-311. Nature of proceeding.

"(a) An abbreviated probate proceeding is a proceeding for probate of a will or a determination of a decedent's intestacy and for the appointment of a personal representative. This proceeding is instituted when an interested person files a petition for an abbreviated probate proceeding with the Court in accordance with the provisions of § 20-304. This proceeding may be conducted without the prior notice required for formal probate under § 20-323.

"(b) The finality of abbreviated probate shall be governed by § 20-331.".

(b) Section 20-312 is amended to read as follows:

"Sec. 20-312. Action on petition.

"(a) General. — Upon a request for abbreviated probate filed in accordance § 20-304, the Court or the Register of Wills shall appoint one or more personal representatives, except as provided in § 20-322. The appointment of a personal representative shall constitute an Order for unsupervised administration, unless the Order specifically provides for supervised administration as provided in § 20-402. In no event, however, shall the appointment of a personal representative be delayed pending the Court's decision with regard to whether the administration will be supervised or unsupervised.

"(b) Wills. — In the case of a petition to admit a will to abbreviated probate, due execution of the will shall be presumed and the Court or the Register of Wills may admit a will to probate either:

"(1) If the will appears to have been duly executed and contains a recital by attesting witnesses of facts constituting due execution; or

"(2) Upon the verified statement of any person with personal knowledge of the circumstances of execution, whether or not the person was in fact an attesting witness, reciting facts showing due execution of the will.

"(c) Additional Proof. — The Register of Wills may require additional verified proof, which shall be filed in the proceeding. If the Register of Wills requires additional proof, the matter may be referred to the Court to determine if one or more personal representatives shall be appointed and whether the will should be admitted to probate.".
